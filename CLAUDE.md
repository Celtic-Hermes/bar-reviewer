# Bar Reviewer — Project Guide for Claude

## What this project is
A mobile web app for reviewing for the **Philippine Bar Exam**. It is a PWA (Progressive Web App): it opens in a phone's browser, can be added to the home screen like a normal app, and **works offline** once loaded.

It is being built by a complete beginner (no coding experience, not a lawyer) for their girlfriend, who will use it to study. She uses an **iPhone**. The builder uses a **Mac with VS Code**.

## How the app should feel
Like a bar prep app:
- She **picks a subject, then a topic**; every round comes **shuffled** (random order, always: there is no in-order option, `startPractice`). A round put back by Continue or a reload keeps its saved order.
- **One question at a time**, full-screen and easy to read on a phone.
- **Mostly essay questions**, plus some multiple choice.
- Questions can link to **related cases** she can open and read.
- **Later (not at first):** a flashcard mode.

### Read the full rule
On a topic or sub-topic screen, **📖 Read the full rule** (under Start practice) appears automatically when **all** of that (sub-)topic's questions share one `source.grNumber` and `data/rules.json` lists a rule with that `number`. (So "All of Special Proceedings", which mixes rules, has no button, and all four Environmental Rules sub-topics open the whole Environmental Rules.) It opens the rule screen: title, number, the pinned highlighter colors, then each section in its own card with its heading on its own bold line (see Section headings, under fill in the blank), and PART / RULE headings between the cards; then the source. The text is downloaded only then, and kept on the phone for offline use.

### Tests (`#screen-tests` "My tests", `openTests` in `tests.js`)
A test per subject, different from practice: she picks what goes in, answers stay hidden until she submits, and she reviews everything at the end. Our own look, on the shared styles. The code lives in `tests.js` (loaded after `app.js`, in `APP_FILES`).
- **Agreed design (the builder's choices):**
  - **Create a test:** checkboxes for topics / sub-topics (with question counts, Select all), question type, how many questions (10 / 20 / 30 / 50 / all, never more than exist), timed or untimed (a suggested limit she can change). Always shuffled.
  - **Question types:** multiple choice and fill in the blank first (fill in the blank is marked by the app: right only if every box is right, at a difficulty picked when making the test, default Key Legal Terms). Essays later (self-rated at review).
  - **Taking it:** one question at a time, no right / wrong shown, time left at the top, flag and skip, Previous / Next, a question map (answered / flagged / not yet; tap to jump), **End test** asks first ("3 unanswered, 2 flagged"). Leaving keeps it in Unfinished exactly where she stopped.
  - **Results and review:** score %, right / wrong / unanswered, time used, a score per sub-topic; then every question with her answer next to the correct one, the explanation and source; filters All / Wrong / Flagged / Unanswered.
  - **Counting:** only on submit, once per answered question, through the same `recordMcq` / `recordFillBlank` as practice (streak, daily goal, mastery, Review missed); unanswered questions count for nothing; the test remembers it was counted, so reviewing never counts again.
  - **Saved** in her progress (`progress.tests`, so in backups; cleaned by its own `clean…` function; old backups simply have none): at most 5 unfinished and the 30 newest finished (older ones dropped, with a note). Only question IDs, answers, flags and times are stored. A question later removed: a finished test keeps its score and shows "No longer in the app"; an unfinished one drops it with a note. Never a crash.
  - **Timer:** pauses whenever the test isn't on screen (app hidden, phone locked, she left the test); time's up = "Time's up" and the test is submitted with what she answered. Unfinished tests live in her progress, so they survive the home-screen timeout.
  - **Way in:** the subject screen's big card (Create a test / Continue test) and its **My tests** card (an app-wide sidebar entry maybe later). The flag = a new outline icon in `icons.js`.
- **Steps:** 1. Tests card + Tests screen (**done**; since then the big card is Create a test / Continue test and the Tests card is My tests). 2. Create a test screen (**done**). 3. Taking a test (multiple choice and fill in the blank, no timer yet: **done**). 4. Flag and question map (**done**). 5. End test + results (and counting) (**done**). 6. Review (**done**). 7. Timer (**done**). 8. Fill in the blank in tests (**done**: it has worked in tests since steps 3–6). 9. Possible later, only if asked: essays in tests (self-rated at review), a Tests entry in the sidebar / ☰ menu.

## Content rules (very important)
The builder is not a lawyer and does not write questions. Claude writes them, so these rules protect accuracy:

1. **Only from the actual decision text.** Every question and case summary must come from the real text of a Supreme Court decision. Never write legal content from memory.
2. **If the text can't be found, stop and say so.** If Claude can't get the full decision for a G.R. number, it tells the builder, who will ask their girlfriend to paste the text. Never fill in the gaps by guessing.
3. **Every question and case has a status:** `"draft"` or `"verified"`. **All new questions start as `"draft"`.** Claude never marks anything `"verified"`. Only a person (the girlfriend or a lawyer) checks a question and asks for the change.
4. **Every question has a source:** case title, G.R. number, the part of the decision it came from (for example, "Ruling, discussion of the second issue"), and an exact quote of that passage so a person can check it quickly. The quote should show the Court's **reasoning**, not just its bottom-line conclusion (especially for "why" or "explain" questions). Skipped stretches are marked with " … "; footnote numbers are left out. Nothing is paraphrased.
5. **Lawyer-provided questions are inspiration, not content.** When a lawyer gives sample questions, Claude writes **new, original** questions that test the same concepts, with different facts and wording, and still based on decision text (rules 1–4). Never copy or lightly reword them.
6. **Plain words.** Case summaries and explanations should be clear and easy to read, while staying faithful to what the decision says.
7. **Class notes are the one exception to rules 1 and 4**, by the builder's decision: questions in `data/class-notes.json` come from the builder's class notes (quoted exactly, obvious misspellings fixed and listed), are always labeled **Notes** (the builder renamed it from "From class notes"; the old "Class notes · file · heading" line under it was dropped as redundant), and never count as official content.
8. **Public-repo reminder.** The GitHub repository and app are public. For now they contain only placeholder content. **Before any real content is added, remind the builder** and talk about whether to keep the app public or make it private.
9. **NotebookLM questions are the second exception**, by the builder's decision: questions in `data/notebooklm.json` come from NotebookLM (fed with her class notes, official texts and reviewers), keep NotebookLM's wording, are always labeled **NotebookLM** (renamed by the builder from "From NotebookLM") and `"draft"`, and never count as official content. Each one is published only after its answer was checked against the official text (LawPhil first) and ends with a **Basis:** line linking that text; anything that can't be confirmed is held back (see **Importing from NotebookLM**).

## Bar exam style (how new questions are asked)
By the builder's decision, every **new** essay and multiple-choice question (official, class notes, and the NotebookLM prompt) is written the way the real Bar asks: the same delivery, the angle the examiners take, and the traps they set. Fill-in-the-blank items are the rule's own text and are not affected. Questions already in the app stay as they are unless the builder asks for a rewrite.
1. **Study real past questions first, never from memory.** Before writing for a subject, read published past Bar questions for it: the Supreme Court's own releases (the official Bar questions and, where published, the examiners' suggested answers) or files the builder provides. Other sites are checked against these where possible; say which source was used. Recent exams count most (the current exam format and syllabus). If no real past questions can be found for a subject, tell the builder before writing, and never claim the questions follow the Bar's style.
2. **Keep notes on what was seen** in `tools/bar-style-notes.md` (one section per subject): which exams were read and their links; how the facts are told and the question is asked (the call: "Decide", "Is X correct? Explain", "What remedy…", several sub-questions); the usual length; the angles tested (which doctrines, from which side); and the traps. Each point names the exam it came from. Reuse the notes next time, and update them when a newer exam is published. The file is made the first time questions are written under this rule. It describes patterns only; it never pastes a past question.
3. **Imitate the style, never the question.** New facts, new names, new wording. Never copy or lightly reword a past Bar question (like content rule 5), and never put a reviewer's or commercial suggested answer into the app or the repository.
4. **The angle:** a realistic fact pattern where she must spot the issue, apply the rule and give the reason, in the form the real questions use (for example, a client's situation and what she would advise; one side's argument and whether it is correct). Multiple choice follows the Bar's own multiple-choice style when the samples have it.
5. **Traps must be fair.** For example: an exception that looks like the general rule, one fact that changes the result, a deadline or a number that matters, the right answer for the wrong reason, or a doctrine that was abandoned or changed. There is still exactly one defensible answer. The explanation (multiple choice) or the suggested answer (essay, in `conclusion` or `keyPoints`) **names the trap** and says how to avoid it ("**Bar trap:** …").
6. **Accuracy comes first.** Past questions guide only the style. The law in every answer still comes from the decision or the official text (content rules 1–4, 7 and 9). An older past question may rest on law that has since changed: check that the rule is current. Never say or suggest that a question is likely to be asked in the exam.
7. **Cases get essays and multiple choice only** (6 essays + 2 or 3 multiple choice, see step 5 of **Adding a new case**), written like the real Bar questions in LawPhil's archive of past Bar questions (https://lawphil.net/courts/bm/barQ/barQs.html). **Never fill in the blank for a case**: by the builder's decision (2026-11-26) fill in the blank is used only when the builder specifically asks for it.
8. **A professor's focus, when the builder gives one, shapes the questions.** **Civil Law → Torts and Quasi-delicts:** the professor stresses **identification, enumeration, principles and their definitions**; cover them the way the real Bar does: short essays ("What is the doctrine of …? Explain.", "What are the requisites of …?", "Distinguish … from …") and identification multiple choice ("This is known as the principle of" with four doctrine names).
9. **Three hypothetical essays per case** (by the builder's decision, 2026-11-27; added on top of the 3 Bar-style essays; then reduced the multiple choice to 2 or 3, so 6 essays + 2–3 multiple choice per new case): a short fact pattern with **new, hypothetical facts** (invented people such as "Ana" and "Ben", and plausible everyday settings), then one direct question such as "Is Ana liable? Explain." or "Is the evidence admissible? Reason briefly." Some may have two short sub-questions (a) and (b). **Never name the case in the question and never ask "what did the Court rule".** The facts must make her recognize the doctrine; a good variation is a pair: one set of facts where the doctrine applies, and a near-miss where it does not. Same parts as every essay (ALAC suggested answer, `keyPoints`, a "**Bar trap:**", and a `source.excerpt` quoting the exact passage the answer rests on).
   - **Accuracy rules (these matter more than the number of questions):** the facts are hypothetical, but the **law** comes only from the decision text and the statute or rule text already in the app. Never invent a doctrine, a section number, a case or a G.R. number, and never stretch a doctrine beyond what the Court actually held. If a doctrine is tied to its particular facts, do not extend it to different facts; write the question only where the decision supports the answer.
   - Cases added before this rule keep their 5 multiple choice (deleting questions would erase her progress) and get the 3 extra essays when the builder asks (done so far for the Damages cases, starting with Mercado v. Lira, case-0078).

## Content format (JSON files)
Administrative cases use their A.C. number in the `grNumber` field (e.g. `"A.C. No. 9514"`).
Content lives in JSON files, separate from the app code:
- **Questions and case summaries live in one file per topic:** `data/content/<subject>/<topic>.json`, e.g. `data/content/mercantile-law/commercial-law.json` (folder and file names: the exact subject and topic names in lower case, with every run of spaces or punctuation turned into `-`). Each file is `{"subject": "…", "topic": "…", "cases": [ … ], "questions": [ … ]}`; every item in it has that same subject and topic, and sub-topics stay inside their topic's file (all of Special Proceedings is one file). Items are kept in ID order.
- `data/catalog.json`: a small summary of all the content, **built by `python3 tools/build-catalog.py`; never edit it by hand.** Run the builder after any change to a topic file (a new topic just needs its new file; the builder lists it). It holds:
  - `topics`: one per topic file (`subject`, `topic`, `file`), in the order the app shows them (subject order, then by each topic's first question ID), each with `subtopics` in the app's order: `{"name": … or null (no sub-topic), "rule": the source.grNumber all its questions share, or null, "essay": [IDs], "mcq": [IDs], "fillblank": [IDs]}`.
  - `cases`: one line per case: `id`, `name`, `grNumber`, `year`, `subject`, `topic`, `subtopic` (if any), `status`, and `questions` (IDs of the questions whose `caseIds` include it).
  - `rules`: one line per rule in `data/rules.json`: `id`, `title`, `number` (its citation), `status`, and the `subject` / `topic` of its first question (null if none). Rules have no year.
  - The app loads only the topic files listed here. For now it still loads all of them when it opens; the home screen's search uses `cases` and `rules`; `topics` is for a planned step (load a topic only when she opens it). About 1.2 MB (150 KB to download) at 3,000 cases.
- **IDs are unique across all topic files**, not just within one. Deleted IDs (q-0001 to q-0003, the old placeholders) are never reused. `python3 tools/check-content.py` prints the next free IDs.
- `data/case-texts/case-0001.txt` …: the full text of each decision, one plain-text file per case, named by case ID. Loaded only when she taps "Read the full text", then saved on her phone for offline use. Copied from the case's `sourceUrl` (LawPhil), with LawPhil's hidden watermarks (like "1awphi1") removed and nothing else changed. Separate opinions are not included.

- `data/syllabus-2027.json`: the official 2027 Bar syllabus (see **2027 Bar Syllabus**): generated from the official PDF, never hand-edited.
- `data/quotes.json`: the home screen's Quote of the day: quotes and reminders (see **Quote of the day**).
- `data/class-notes.json`: questions made from class notes, labeled **Notes** (see **Class-notes questions** under the content checker). Built by a private script; never hand-edited.
- `data/rules.json`: a list of rule documents whose full text she can read (`id` like `rule-0001`, `status`, `title`, `number` = the same text as the items' `source.grNumber`, e.g. `"A.M. No. 07-9-12-SC"` or `"Rules of Court, Rule 91"`, and `sourceUrl`).
- `data/rule-texts/rule-0001.txt` …: the full text of each rule, one plain-text file per rule, loaded only when she taps **📖 Read the full rule**, then saved on her phone for offline use (same as case texts). It runs from the rule's title through its last section (not the resolution approving it, not signatures). Paragraphs are separated by a blank line. A section starts with `SECTION 1.` / `Section 1.` / `SEC. 1.` / `Sec. 1.` (or `ARTICLE`/`Article`/`ART.`/`Art.` + number + period); a heading between sections starts with `PART` / `RULE` / `TITLE` / `CHAPTER` / `BOOK` + a number, with its title on the next line of the same paragraph (e.g. `RULE 91\nEscheats`). Every fill-in-the-blank item's `fullText` from that rule must appear in it word for word.

**IDs never change once created** (like `q-0001`, `case-0001`). The app saves her progress by ID, so changing an ID would lose her history for that question.

### The Basis box (explanations and essay conclusions)
When a multiple-choice or fill-in-the-blank `explanation`, or an essay's `suggestedAnswer.conclusion`, contains a line `**Basis:** <citation>. Checked against the official text: <url>[, <url>…].` (the class-notes and NotebookLM questions: 162 today), the app takes that line out of the displayed text and shows a small **Basis** box after it (`parseBasis` → `{before, citation, urls, after}`, `makeBasisBox`, `setTextWithBasis` in `app.js`; `.basis-box` in `style.css`): the heading "Basis", the citation in plain words (no web address), the question's optional **`basisExcerpt`** (a short exact supporting quote from the official text, shown as an indented muted quote; see **Basis excerpts** below), and a **Read the basis** button per link (the shared `btn btn-secondary btn-small`, new tab, `rel="noopener"`; with several links each is named from its address, "Read the basis: R.A. 7042", `basisLinkName`, else "(1)", "(2)"). Offline the buttons are replaced by "You're offline. This needs internet." and come back by themselves (`showLookupState`, shared with Look it up online). Anything after the links (" **Beyond notes.**") shows as a small line in the box; a Basis line with no link shows the citation only. Used on the practice question screen (MCQ explanation, fill-in-the-blank Discussion, essay Conclusion) and in tests' Review answers. **The data is never changed**; text without a Basis line (or not in this shape) shows exactly as before.
- **Basis excerpts (`basisExcerpt`, step 2, started 2026-11-20; one official source at a time):** an optional plain string on a question: 1–3 sentences (ideally under ~60 words, never 120 or more) copied **exactly** from the official text its Basis cites (the statute's own wording, spelling and typos kept; only whitespace collapsed); never from the notes, a reviewer or memory. At most 2 pieces, in the official text's order, joined with " … "; a piece cut out of a longer sentence may start with "… " or end with " …". No `**`, no web address. **Done so far: R.A. 8792** (48 of the 49 class-notes questions that cite it; pq-0014 waits for the Rules on Electronic Evidence, which state its answer). Next: add the next official file key to `EXCERPT_SOURCES` (RA 11967, the Rules on Electronic Evidence, RA 11659, the cases), then the NotebookLM sources.
  - **Class notes:** made by `make_questions.py` (`EXCERPT_SOURCES`, `excerpt_for(key, phrases)`: the full sentence(s) of `sources/official/<key>.txt` holding the question's check phrases from `V`, cut to the clause around the phrase if longer than 60 words; `EXCERPT_OVERRIDE` = a better passage picked by hand from the same official text, with a comment saying why; `EXCERPT_SKIP` = cited but no quote yet, with the reason). The build fails if any piece isn't found word for word (in order, whitespace-normalized) in the official copy, or the excerpt is too long, has more than 2 pieces, `**` or a URL. NotebookLM excerpts (later) go through `tools/import-notebooklm.py`.
  - **Checker** (`check_basis_excerpt` in `tools/check-content.py`, for official, class-notes and NotebookLM questions; never crashes): non-empty string, under 120 words, no `**`, no URL; every piece, in order, in the official copy its Basis links to (`sources/official/`, mapped by `OFFICIAL_URL_KEYS` or the URL's file name; official questions may also quote their linked case or rule text): **PROBLEM** if that copy is on this Mac and the quote isn't in it, **CHECK** if no copy is here.
  - **Privacy exemption:** the notes often quote the same statute, so an excerpt can share 8–12+ words with them. A `basisExcerpt` is exempt from the 8-words-from-the-notes check (class notes, NotebookLM) and from the 12-word privacy checks (`check_privacy`, `tools/check-public.py`, via `strip_official_excerpts`) **only when** every piece is verified in an official copy in `sources/official/`: it is then public-domain law text, not the notes. Any excerpt not found there is checked like everything else. Never put notes text into an excerpt.

### Fill-in-the-blank item (for memorizing exact rule text)
```json
{
  "id": "q-0056",
  "type": "fillblank",
  "status": "draft",
  "subject": "Remedial Law",
  "topic": "Special Proceedings",
  "subtopic": "Writ of Amparo",
  "prompt": "Section 4. ... The petition may be filed on any day and at any ____ ...",
  "fullText": "Section 4. ... The petition may be filed on any day and at any time ...",
  "caseIds": [],
  "source": { "caseTitle": "The Rule on the Writ of Amparo", "grNumber": "A.M. No. 07-9-12-SC", "url": "...", "part": "Section 4", "excerpt": "..." }
}
```
- Blanks are written as **four or more underscores** (`____`). `fullText` is the exact same text with nothing blanked; everything outside the blanks must match it character for character, so the app can bold the missing words when she taps **Show answer**. Don't put two blanks right next to each other, and don't use `**` in either field.
- Blank the legally significant words (required elements, periods/deadlines, standards, who may file, where), not random words. **Never blank the section heading** ("SECTION 1. Petition."): it's the clue (the checker reports it as a PROBLEM).
- **Section headings on their own line** (everywhere a section is shown: Quiz, Show answer, Read, and a rule's full text). `splitSectionHeading` in `app.js` (used by `richText`, so every present and future section gets it automatically) finds the heading at the start of the text: **SECTION / Section / SEC. / Sec. / ARTICLE / Article / ART. / Art. + number + period, then up to the first period that is followed by a dash** (-, – or —, with or without a space). The heading is shown as its own row (`.section-heading`): the label ("SEC. 4.", `.section-label`) and the title ("No Docket Fees", `.section-title`), both bold in the normal text color (`--text`) so the whole heading is one color (the title's final period is kept in the text but not shown), then a thin divider line; the dash and the spaces or blank lines after it are kept but never shown (`.section-sep`), and the content starts on the next line. A text that doesn't start that way is shown as before (the checker gives a CHECK). The heading's words are never picked as blanks, and "X of Y words hidden" counts only the content (`sectionContent`). The label/title split (`SECTION_LABEL`): the label is SECTION/Section/SEC./Sec./ARTICLE/… + number + period, the title is the rest. The shared styles live only in the `.section-heading` / `.section-label` / `.section-title` / `.section-sep` rules in `style.css`. In Quiz, changing the difficulty never redraws heading words (`applyFbLevel` skips them).
  - **Word numbers never change** (her highlights): the hidden dash still counts as a word, as before. Where a dash is glued to the heading's last word ("file.-", "pre-trial.-(a)"), that word keeps its one number and the glued part is shown without a number of its own. The rule screen passes `headingEndsWord` (its numbering always counted the heading's end as a word end, from when it bolded headings with `**`).
- **Names on screen vs internal IDs:** every word she sees for these controls is defined once in `app.js` (the "WORDS SHE SEES" block: `FB_LEVEL_NAMES`, `FB_LEVEL_LABELS`, `FB_TEXT`, put on the page by `showFbLabels`): **Difficulty** (the dropdown), **Warm-up (2–3 blanks)**, **Key Legal Terms (~20%)**, **Steady (~35%)**, **Deep dive (~55%)**, **Word for word (~75%)**, **↻ Shuffle blanks**, **👁 Hold to peek**. The internal level IDs (`light`, `key`, `moderate`, `intensive`, `verbatim`) are saved in her progress and used by the mastery rule (`MASTERY_FB_LEVELS` in progress.js): never rename them; to rename a level, change only its words there. (The home screen doesn't explain the mastery rule; it still follows `MASTERY_FB_LEVELS`.)
- **How it's practiced:** a **Difficulty** dropdown above the text picks how much is hidden (more hidden = harder). The blanks written in the content are the "key legal terms":
  - **Warm-up** (internal ID `light`): 2–3 of the key legal terms (up to 3, picked at random among the key terms that have at least one non-filler word; all of them if the item has fewer).
  - **Key Legal Terms** (~20%): all the key legal terms, nothing else.
  - **Filler words (Warm-up and Key Legal Terms only):** inside a key term, the words in `FB_FILLER_WORDS` (one list at the top of the fill-in-the-blank code in `app.js`, meant to be edited: articles, conjunctions, prepositions and words like "any", "shall", "other") are never blanked; they show as plain text (`.fb-shown`) and only the meaningful words get boxes ("docket and other lawful fees" → docket, lawful, fees). Steady, Deep dive and Word for word ignore the list. No key term in the current content is made only of filler words (every one keeps at least one box).
  - **Steady** (~35%), **Deep dive** (~55%), **Word for word** (~75%) (internal IDs `moderate`, `intensive`, `verbatim`): all the key legal terms, plus other words picked at random until about that share of the words is hidden. "SEC."/"Section", list numbers like "(a)", and words without letters are never picked.
  - **↻ Shuffle blanks** (just under the dropdown) hides a different set of words at the same level (never the same set again if any other is possible). What she typed stays in boxes that are still hidden. It's grayed out when it can't change anything: at Key Legal Terms (always all the key terms), at Warm-up when the item has 3 key terms or fewer, after Show answer, or when there's no other word to pick. Tapping it while grayed out (before Show answer) shows a short note saying why.
  - The dropdown starts at the level she used last (saved in `barReviewer.fbLevel.v1`, not part of backups) and can be changed until **Show answer**. A note under the text says "X of Y words hidden (Z%)".
  - Every hidden word is a **text box** she types into (shown words stay plain text). **One box per word:** a key-term blank that stands for several words ("indigent petitioner") gets one box for each word (`blankParts`; box keys "12.0", "12.1" … = word 12 of the prompt, part 0, 1 …; a plain word's box is "12"), each word's punctuation outside its box, each box checked on its own. Boxes grow as she types; an empty box is always the same size, so it doesn't hint at the answer's length. The Return key moves to the next box. What she types is kept if she changes the level (for words still hidden) and after a reload (`fbTyped` in the saved spot).
  - **Live checking (Quiz):** as soon as a box holds the right word (compared like Show answer: capitals, punctuation, spaces and accents don't matter), it turns green (`.fb-right`) and the cursor jumps to the next box that isn't green (`focusNextOpenBox`; none left: the keyboard closes). Punctuation or a space typed right after that word doesn't spill into the next box (`justArrived`). Leaving a box (Return/Next, Tab, tapping elsewhere) with a wrong word turns it red (`.fb-wrong`) without showing the right word; an empty box stays neutral; typing in a red box clears the red at once (`liveCheckBox`). "You typed X of Y blanks correctly" shows in Quiz from the start and updates per word (`updateTypedCount`; the note's first part is kept in `data-base`). After a reload or a level change, boxes with the right word start green (red comes back only when she leaves a box again). The same `.fb-right` / `.fb-wrong` styles as Show answer. Nothing here is saved: ratings and mastery are unchanged.
  - **👁 Hold to peek** (just above Show answer, Quiz only): while she holds it (finger, mouse, or Space/Enter), each box is hidden and its answer shows in its place (dotted underline); letting go, sliding off, or switching apps hides them again. What she typed stays in the boxes. Nothing is scored or saved. It disappears after Show answer and in Read mode.
  - **Show answer** marks each box **green** (right) or **red** (wrong or empty), ignoring capital letters, punctuation, spaces and accents, says "You typed X of Y blanks correctly" (counted per box, so per word), locks the boxes, and reveals the full text with every hidden word bold and underlined, each word marked on its own (`markedAnswer`). The boxes' green/red result is never saved: her score, Review missed, daily count and mastery use only her rating and the level, so one-box-per-word changed none of them. The typed result is not saved to her score; only her own rating is. She then rates **Got it / Missed it**: Missed it goes into Review missed; per subject the app keeps `fbCorrect` of `fbAnswered` ("X of Y correct"). The level picked doesn't affect the score.
- **Quiz / Read switch** at the top of every fill-in-the-blank item. **Read** shows that item's `fullText` as plain reading text (`reading-text` class, justified): no blanks, no boxes, nothing graded (Previous / Next are always available, as on every question). Each item is one section, so sections stay separate (one per screen). Read stays on for the rest of the round (saved as `fbView` in the round); a new round starts in Quiz. Switching back to Quiz finds the quiz exactly as she left it. In Read, everything marked `fb-quiz-only` in `index.html` is hidden by one rule in `style.css`. If a round ends with nothing scored, the results screen says so.
- Each word stays one "word" for highlighting whether it's hidden or not, so her highlights don't move between levels. Changing an item's `prompt` (which words are blanked) can still shift them.
- Practice on the subject screen always uses every question type (the old Question type picker was removed); Create a test has its own **Multiple choice / Fill in the blank / Both** choice.

## Adding a new case (default procedure)
Whenever the builder asks to add a new case, follow this checklist without being reminded. The content rules above still apply to every step.

1. **Announce the IDs first.** Before starting, tell the builder the next free case ID and the question IDs that will be used (the next 8 or 9 `q-` numbers: 6 essays + 2–3 multiple choice). Get them from the "Next free IDs" line of `python3 tools/check-content.py`; never reuse or renumber an ID.
2. **Get the real decision text.** Fetch it from a reliable source (LawPhil, the Supreme Court E-Library, etc.), never from memory. If it can't be fetched, stop and ask the builder to have the text pasted in (content rule 2).
3. **Save the full text** as `data/case-texts/<case-id>.txt` (plain text, UTF-8):
   - Only the main decision: from the court header through the signatures and footnotes. Leave out site menus and the list of separate opinions (those aren't included).
   - Keep the paragraphs (blank line between them), and write footnote numbers as `[1]`, `[2]`… both in the text and at the start of each footnote under a `Footnotes` heading (the number is glued to the word before it, like `Certiorari[1]`; a bracketed number after a space, like "eighty [180] days", is the decision's own wording and the checker ignores it).
   - Remove LawPhil's hidden watermarks (like `1awphi1`, `ℒαwρhi৷`, `1âwphi1`): search for odd tokens and report how many were removed. Change nothing else, typos included.
   - Check every footnote number in the text has a matching footnote, and tell the builder the file's size.
4. **Add the case summary** to the `cases` list of its topic file (`data/content/<subject>/<topic>.json`; a new topic gets a new file plus a line in `data/catalog.json`): `facts`, `issue`, `ruling`, `keyDoctrine` in plain words, faithful to the decision, `status: "draft"`, under the correct subject (one of the 8 exact names above) and topic. Privacy and communication cases go under **Mercantile Law → Communication Law**; reuse an existing topic name when one fits, and say so when creating a new topic. `sourceUrl` is where the text came from.
5. **Write exactly 6 essay questions and 2 or 3 multiple-choice questions**, all `status: "draft"`, all with `caseIds` linking the new case, written like the real Bar questions (see **Bar exam style**): 3 Bar-style essays as before, plus **3 hypothetical fact-pattern essays** (Bar exam style item 9: invented people and facts, one direct question, never naming the case, law only from the decision). Some essays may be short define, enumerate or distinguish questions, and some multiple choice may be identification items ("This is known as the principle of …"). **No fill in the blank** for a case unless the builder asks:
   - Essays use the ALAC suggested answer (`answer`, `legalBasis`, `application`, `conclusion`) plus `keyPoints`.
   - Multiple choice: exactly 4 choices (A–D), one correct, and an explanation of why it's right and the others wrong.
   - Lawyer-provided samples are inspiration only (content rule 5).
   - Write them following **Bar exam style** (above): read the subject's notes in `tools/bar-style-notes.md` (or the real past questions first), and name the trap in each answer.
6. **Source excerpts show the reasoning.** Each `source.excerpt` is an exact quote of the decision, long enough to show the Court's actual reasoning, not just a one-line conclusion (especially for "why" or "explain" questions). Skipped stretches are marked with ` … `, and the pieces must be **in the same order as in the decision** (otherwise Find in case can't highlight them); footnote numbers are left out. Before finishing, check that every piece of every excerpt appears word for word in the full-text file, and that `source.part` names where it came from.
7. **Follow the text formatting conventions** in all content fields:
   - `**word**` for bold.
   - Numbered points as `(1) … (2) … (3)` (or `(a) (b)`), starting right after a sentence end, colon or semicolon. The app then shows each point on its own line with a bold number. Points written mid-sentence ("between (a) X and (b) Y") stay inline.
8. **Test before committing:** `python3 tools/build-catalog.py` (rebuilds the catalog, so the new case shows up in the home screen's search), then `python3 tools/check-content.py` says "All content OK"; the app loads without errors, the case opens with its summary and full text, all the questions show up under the right subject and topic, and "Find in case" finds each question's excerpt in the full text.
9. **Cache version:** bump `CACHE_VERSION` in `sw.js` only if an app file changed (see Technical choices). Adding a case normally changes only data files, so no bump.
10. **Commit and report:** one commit for the case (or one per step if it was split), then tell the builder what was added, the IDs used, the full-text file size, and anything that needs a person to check.

## Adding a new rule (fill-in-the-blank, default procedure)
For a procedural rule or a Rules of Court rule (like the Rule on the Writ of Amparo), memorized as fill-in-the-blank items. Follow this checklist without being reminded; the content rules above still apply to every step.

1. **Announce first.** Tell the builder which rule is being done (name, A.M. or Rule number), the source link, and the next free `q-` ID. Get it from the "Next free IDs" line of `python3 tools/check-content.py`; never reuse or renumber an ID. Do **one rule at a time**: one rule = one commit = one save point.
2. **Get the real text from the source.** Use the link the builder gave, or an official source (LawPhil, the Supreme Court website or E-Library). Never from memory. A third-party site (study sites, blogs) must be cross-checked against an official source; if they differ, use the official text and tell the builder what differed. If the text can't be fetched, stop and ask for it to be pasted (content rule 2).
3. **Confirm the structure before writing anything.** Count the sections and tell the builder the count and the question IDs it will use (one item per section, consecutive). If the rule has several parts (for example, separate writs or remedies in one A.M.), describe how it's organized and ask whether it should be one sub-topic or several before writing.
4. **Clean the text, change nothing else.** Decode the page with its own character set (LawPhil pages are often `windows-1252`) so dashes and quotes come out right. Remove LawPhil's hidden watermarks (like `1awphi1`) and report how many were removed. Keep everything else exactly as published: wording, typos, dashes, capitals. Paragraphs and list items are separated by a blank line. Only the rule's own sections go in, not the resolution that approves it. **Page-break leftovers** from LawPhil's conversion of a PDF (a sentence or word split by a paragraph break, or a printed page header like "Rules of Procedure for Environmental Cases" pasted mid-sentence) are repaired by rejoining the sentence with a single space; report each one to the builder, item by item.
5. **One `fillblank` item per section**, in order, all `status: "draft"`:
   - `subject` / `topic` / `subtopic`: where the builder asked (e.g. Remedial Law → Special Proceedings → the rule's own sub-topic). Add the new sub-topic to "Topics so far" above, with its ID range.
   - `fullText`: the whole section exactly, starting with its heading ("Section 3. Where to file petition. - …").
   - `prompt`: the same text with the legally significant words blanked (`____`), following the fill-in-the-blank rules above: required elements, periods/deadlines, standards, who may file, where, what is or isn't allowed. Never two blanks with only spaces between them.
   - `caseIds: []`. `source`: `caseTitle` = the rule's official title, `grNumber` = the A.M. number or "Rules of Court, Rule 91", `url` = the source link, `part` = "Section N (Heading)", `excerpt` = the whole section text (same as `fullText`).
6. **Save the whole rule for "Read the full rule".** Add an entry to `data/rules.json` (next free `rule-` ID; `number` exactly equal to the items' `source.grNumber`) and save the cleaned text as `data/rule-texts/<rule-id>.txt` in the format described under Content format. Tell the builder the file's size. Any sub-topic whose items all come from this one rule then shows the button automatically; nothing else needs setting up.
7. **Check before committing:** `python3 tools/build-catalog.py` (rebuilds the catalog, so the new rule shows up in the home screen's search), then `python3 tools/check-content.py` says "All content OK" (it checks most of what follows); every `fullText` appears word for word in the cleaned source text and in the rule's text file; every `prompt` lines up with its `fullText` (same text outside the blanks, so Show answer bolds the right words); the IDs are consecutive and unused; the JSON is valid.
8. **Test in the app:** the sub-topic shows up under the right subject and topic with the right item count; Quiz, Read, the Difficulty levels and Show answer work on a few items; **📖 Read the full rule** opens the whole rule, one card per section.
9. **Cache version:** data-only changes need no bump (see Technical choices).
10. **Commit and report:** one commit for the rule, then tell the builder the section count, the IDs used, the source, how many watermarks were removed, and anything a person should check (for example, odd wording in the source).

- **Views:** a **Month / Week** switch (the shared `.seg-switch` / `.seg-btn` look, the same as the Quiz/Read switch); Month is the default, and her last choice is remembered on this device (`barReviewer.scheduleView.v1`, not backed up). ‹ / › move a month or a week; **Today** comes back. Weeks run Sunday to Saturday (`WEEK_STARTS_ON`).
  - **Month on a wide screen (700px+):** a calendar (`showMonthGrid`): each day shows its number (today's filled; tap it to see that day below the calendar), a **+** (add a task), 🌴 for a day off, and up to 4 task chips (checkbox, title, time; "+N more" shows the day below); an extra column gives each week's total ("6 tasks, 1 done").
  - **Month on a phone:** the same month as a vertical list of its days, with a "Week of Nov 15 · 4 tasks, 1 done" line before each week.
  - **Week:** the 7 days as a list.
- **Top:** "N days until exam (date)" from Settings' exam date (never made up), or a **Set your exam date in Settings ›** link; a bar with "X / Y tasks completed in <month>" (or "this week"). A day off's hidden tasks don't count.
- **Tasks** (`makeTaskCard`; `.task-card` in lists, `.task-chip` in the calendar): checkbox (completed = crossed out and softer), title, optional time ("30m", "1h 30m"), on its **type's soft color** (`--type-q` … `--type-o`, always with dark text; the type and subject are also written under the title in lists, so color isn't the only clue). Tapping a task edits it; tapping a linked title opens the link. Types: Questions, Case reading, Fill-in-the-blank, Review, Exam, Other (`TASK_TYPES`; stored as `y`: q, c, f, r, e; Other = nothing stored, also for tasks saved before types existed).
- **Add / edit** (`openTaskDialog`, `#task-backdrop`; **+ Add task** in lists, **+** in calendar days): the shared dialog look and setting rows: **Title** (required, up to 120 characters), **Type**, **Subject** (optional), **Time** (optional, 1–999 minutes), **Opens** (optional link). Editing keeps a task completed. **Delete this task** (in the edit dialog) deletes at once, with the shared Undo bar for 10 seconds (Undo puts it back in its place). At most 50 tasks a day.
- **Day off** (🌴, a toggle in each day's heading, `.day-off-btn`): the day is shaded lavender and holds no tasks (no + Add task). Switching it on for a day that has tasks asks first (`askConfirm`); the tasks are only hidden, never deleted, and come back when Day off is switched off.
- **Saved** in `progress.schedule` (so it's in backups), a day only if it has something, with short keys (`k` tasks, `t` title, `m` minutes, `d` done, `y` type, `s` subject, `l` link, `off` Day off); `cleanSchedule` (progress.js) drops anything it doesn't understand, on load, in backups and on restore. Read and write only through `scheduleDay(date)` / `setScheduleDay(date, tasks, off)`.
- **Links:** the dialog's **Opens** row (`#task-link`, built by `fillTaskLinkChoices` from the current content): None (default), a whole subject (`"subject:<name>"`), a topic (`"topic:<subject>|<topic>"`), a sub-topic (`"topic:<subject>|<topic>|<sub-topic>"`, e.g. Special Proceedings › Writ of Amparo), a case (`"case:<case-id>"`, opens the case screen) or a rule (`"rule:<rule-id>"`, opens Read the full rule). A linked title opens it (`taskLinkTarget`); from a case or rule opened this way, Back returns to the schedule (`caseCameFrom` / `ruleCameFrom` = "schedule", also after a reload). Single fill-in-the-blank items aren't offered (200+ of them); a subject or topic screen's own Back follows the usual subject path. A link to something no longer in the app is treated as no link (the title edits) and shows in the dialog as "(no longer in the app)", kept unless she changes it.
- Nothing is ever planned automatically, and there's no drag and drop.

## Settings
A **Settings** screen (`#screen-settings`), reached from the ⚙️ button on the home screen (phones, next to ☰), the **Settings** entry in the sidebar / ☰ menu, or the home screen's "done today" tile. **‹ Back** returns to exactly where she was (`settingsCameFrom`; the screen she left is only hidden meanwhile, so a question keeps its state). Settings is never saved as her spot, so a reload there puts her back where she was before it (an open practice round is never lost).
- **Study:** **Daily goal** (10 / 20 / 30 / 50, default 20, `settings.dailyGoal`) and **Exam date** (optional, empty until she picks one, `settings.examDate` as "YYYY-MM-DD", with a Clear button; "Used later for the study schedule").
- **Data:** **Back up my progress** and **Restore progress** (same code as before: `#backup-btn`, `#restore-input`, `#backup-message`), each with a one-line explanation. After a restore, the Settings controls show the restored values (`fillSettings`).
- **Display:** **Show quote of the day** (a switch, the shared `.switch`; `settings.showQuote`, on by default; `showQuote()` / `setShowQuote()` in progress.js). When it's off, the home screen shows the old streak line under the greeting instead of the quote.
- **About:** the "About these questions" notice (AI-assisted study drafts; check the official text), always there even after it's dismissed on Home, and a line that fill-in-the-blank items from a rule, and the full rules, are the rule's own text as published.
- Every setting is one shared **setting row** (`.setting-row`: `.setting-text` with `.setting-label` + `.setting-desc`, then `.setting-control`; `.setting-control-wide` for a full-width button), inside a `.settings-card` under a `.settings-group` heading.
- Values live in `progress.settings`, so they're in every backup. `cleanSettings` (progress.js) keeps only known settings with valid values, on load, in backups and on restore (`restoreProgress` cleans them too); read them only through `settingsObject()` and a getter with a default (`dailyGoal()`, `examDate()`), so missing or old values never crash anything.

### Adding a new setting (checklist)
1. **Pick its group** (Study, Data, Display…; a new group = a new `<h2 class="label settings-group">` + `<section class="card settings-card">` in `#screen-settings`).
2. **Add one `.setting-row`** in `index.html`: a `.setting-label` (a `<label for>` the control), a one-line `.setting-desc`, and the control in `.setting-control` (a `<select>`, `<input>` or button; text-like controls 16px or more so the iPhone doesn't zoom).
3. **Store it in `progress.settings`** (progress.js): add a line to `cleanSettings` that keeps it only if valid, a getter that returns a sensible default when it's missing (always via `settingsObject()`), and a setter that calls `saveProgress()`.
4. **Wire it up** in `app.js`: show the saved value in `fillSettings()`, and a `change` listener that calls the setter (and refreshes anything that shows it).
5. Update the `settings:` line at the top of progress.js and this section of CLAUDE.md.
6. **Test:** change it, reload (it stays), back up and restore (it comes back), an old backup without it (the default, no crash), offline, light and dark mode.
7. Bump `CACHE_VERSION` (app files changed).

Notes saved before formatting existed have only `text` and are turned into `html` on load (their characters stay literal text, never formatting).
  - **Safety:** `cleanNoteHtml` (in `progress.js`) rebuilds any HTML from scratch keeping only those four things: every attribute is dropped, other tags keep only their text, and scripts, styles, pictures, frames etc. are dropped entirely (it reads the HTML with DOMParser, which never runs anything). The buttons apply the style to the selection (tap again to remove it) and show as pressed when the cursor is in text with that style. They cancel `mousedown`/`pointerdown`/`touchstart` so the box keeps the focus: on an iPhone the keyboard stays open and the selection stays. On a computer, **Cmd+B / Cmd+I / Cmd+U** (Mac) or **Ctrl+B / Ctrl+I / Ctrl+U** (Windows and others; on a Mac, Ctrl+B is left alone because it moves the cursor back) do the same thing through the same `applyNoteFormat`, so the buttons light up the same way; the browser's own action for those keys is cancelled. The cleaner accepts every way browsers write these styles (`<b>`/`<strong>`/`font-weight` bold, bolder or 600–900; `<i>`/`<em>`/`font-style` italic or oblique; `<u>`/`text-decoration(-line)` underline) and saves them as `<b>`, `<i>`, `<u>`. `showNotesBox(box, id)` loads a note. It saves by itself half a second after she stops typing, and immediately when the box loses focus, when any screen changes (`showScreen` calls `saveAllNotes`), and when the app is hidden. A note cleared to blank is removed. The box grows with the text (no scrolling inside it) and uses 17px text so the iPhone doesn't zoom in. Boxes: on the case screen after Key doctrine (`case-notes`), and on the rule screen under the title (`rule-notes`), so every rule, including ones added later, has one.

The box grows with the text (no scrolling inside it) and uses 17px text so the iPhone doesn't zoom in.

A question counts once each time she finishes it: a multiple-choice answer submitted, or an essay / fill-in-the-blank rated (any difficulty). Read mode, Peek and just opening a question don't count. A **streak day** = a day with at least one.
  - `results`: per question ID, `{ "last": "right"|"wrong"|"got"|"partly"|"missed", "on": date, "inARow": n, "level": difficulty level ID (fill in the blank only) }`.
  - `inARow` (right answers in a row): **+1** for a correct multiple-choice answer, an essay Got it, or a fill-in-the-blank Got it at **Steady, Deep dive or Word for word**; **reset to 0** by a wrong answer, Partly or Missed it (any level); **unchanged** by a fill-in-the-blank Got it at Warm-up or Key Legal Terms (still counts for the day). It goes up **at most once per day**: `countedOn` is the day it last went up, and another right answer that same day leaves it unchanged (after a reset, the first right answer starts it at 1 again, even the same day). So **mastered = `inARow` of 2 or more = right on two different days in a row, with nothing wrong in between.**

## Rules for working on this project
**Changes to the app itself** (removing, redesigning or restyling screens and sections, and the code behind them) go to the **app-agent** (`.claude/agents/app-agent.md`, this Mac only): it proposes the steps first, builds only after the builder's OK, keeps her saved data safe, updates this guide and bumps the cache version, and never touches content in `data/` or commits.

These rules matter more than speed.

1. **Explain every change in simple terms.**
   - Assume no coding knowledge. Avoid jargon; if a technical word is needed, explain it in one plain sentence.
   - Say *what* changed, *why*, and *which files* were touched.
2. **Make one small change at a time.**
   - Each step should do one thing the builder can see or check.
   - Don't bundle unrelated changes. Don't add features that weren't asked for.
   - If a task is big, split it into small steps and list them before starting.
3. **Always say how to run and test it.**
   - Give step-by-step instructions: what to type, what to open, what to tap, and what should appear if it worked.
   - Include how to check it on her iPhone (Safari) and in offline mode when relevant.
   - Test it yourself first where possible, and say honestly if something couldn't be tested.
4. **Commit to Git after each working step.**
   - Only commit once the step works.
   - Use a short, plain-English commit message (e.g. "Show one essay question with its suggested answer").
   - Tell the builder a save point was made and that it's possible to go back to it.
5. **When something wasn't specified, pick the simplest reasonable option and explain why.**

## Technical choices
- **Plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.** No frameworks or build steps unless there's a clear reason, explained first. This keeps things simple to run and fix.
- **Phone first**, especially iPhone Safari. It should still work on a computer.
- **System areas (status bar, home indicator, notch): the page header keeps `viewport-fit=cover`, and everything that touches a screen edge uses four shared variables** `--safe-top` / `--safe-right` / `--safe-bottom` / `--safe-left` (`env(safe-area-inset-*, 0px)`, defined once in `:root`; **never write `env()` anywhere else**). They are 0 in a normal browser tab, so nothing changes there. Used by: the page padding (all four edges), the ☰ and ⚙ buttons, the floating sidebar and the menu drawer, the Undo bar, dialogs, the sticky highlighter palette, the Find in case panels, the case side panel.
  - **The solid strip** (`body::before`): a fixed strip as tall as the status bar (`--safe-top`), in the page color (`--bg`, in both light and dark mode), above the page, sidebar and menu (z-index 70) but below the Undo bar and dialogs. iPadOS tints and blurs whatever is under the status bar (a navy sidebar there made a fuzzy grey fade), so now that is plain page background. `html` also has `--bg` as its background.
  - **No fixed element may touch the top edge of the screen** (the sidebar is a floating panel starting at `--safe-top` + `--sidebar-gap`, 12px). `theme-color` (two metas, by color scheme) and the manifest's `theme_color` are the page color (`#f4f1ea` light, `#15171b` dark), so the browser bar matches. `apple-mobile-web-app-status-bar-style` stays `default`; the status bar is never hidden.
  - **Testing it:** in a headless browser, emulate the system areas (the DevTools protocol's `Emulation.setSafeAreaInsetsOverride`: top 24 / bottom 20 for an iPad, top 47 / bottom 34 for an iPhone, left and right 47 for an iPhone sideways) and check that every pixel in the top strip is the page color. On the iPad, a Home Screen app keeps the manifest it had when it was added: after changing the manifest, remove the icon and add it again (back up her progress first: see below).
- **Layout system** (one shared set of sizes for every screen; variables in `:root` at the top of `style.css`, explained in the comment block there):
  - **Spacing:** `--space-1` 4px · `--space-2` 8px · `--space-3` 12px · `--space-4` 16px · `--space-5` 24px · `--space-6` 32px · `--space-7` 48px. **Corners:** `--radius-sm` 8px (badges, small boxes) · `--radius-md` 12px (buttons, inputs, chips) · `--radius-lg` 16px (cards, tiles) · `--radius-pill` 999px.
  - **Page edges:** `--gutter` (`clamp(16px, 4vw, 40px)`) on the left and right of `.page`, plus the `--safe-*` variables; the ☰ button sits on the same edge.
  - **Two page widths** (`max-width` includes the gutters): reading screens `.page-read` = `--w-read` (720px: on a wide screen the text column is 640px), list screens `.page-list` = `--w-list` (1200px), centered next to the sidebar. `showScreen` sets the class from **`PAGE_KIND` in `app.js`** (one line per screen; not listed = read). **List** (only screens with a real grid of cards, which use the width well): Home, Subject, Study Schedule. **Read** (everything single-column, so nothing is stretched just to fill space): sub-topics, topic, Tagged, My tests, Create a test, Test results, Review answers, Search, 2027 Bar Syllabus, a question, practice results, taking a test, case, rule, My notes, Settings, For clarification. A screen moves to List only when its layout gets a grid that needs the room. The one documented exception: the case screen from 1280px is a reading page plus its 368px side panel.
